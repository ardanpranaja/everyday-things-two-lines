# Candidate two-line entries

Competition: Everyday Things, Two Lines, Longer Run.
Constraint: exactly two lines, 1–400 characters total, not only white
space. Line 1 explains one everyday thing to a 10-year-old in a single
vivid sentence built on one analogy. Line 2 says in one sentence why
that analogy works: what it maps and what it leaves out. The judge
weighs the everyday explanation first, then how concrete and surprising
the analogy is, then how honest and specific the second line is.

## Candidates

1. A toaster is a tiny sunbed for bread: two slices lie back under glowing wires to catch a tan, then pop up once they're golden brown.
   The sunbed picture works because both brown something with steady heat until a timer calls time, but it leaves out that bread can't choose its tan and the glow comes from electric wires, not the sun.

2. Rain is the sky wringing out a huge grey sponge over your head.
   The sponge fits because clouds hold water and let it fall when they're full, but it leaves out that clouds aren't solid at all — rain is tiny drops forming as warm damp air cools, not a squeeze.

3. A traffic jam is a river of cars that forgot how to flow, every boat bumper to bumper and going nowhere fast.
   The river fits because everyone drifts the same way at the same slow crawl, but it leaves out that rivers have no drivers choosing lanes and no crash or too many boats causing the stop.

4. A queue is a caterpillar made of people, shuffling forward one tiny step at a time until the front peels off.
   The caterpillar fits because everyone stands in one long body moving at the same slow pace, but it leaves out that caterpillar legs all move together while people in a queue can talk, leave, or swap places.

5. An elevator is a little room that takes the stairs for you, swallowing you on one floor and spitting you out on another.
   The room fits because it carries you up and down without you climbing, but it leaves out the stairs entirely — there are no steps inside, just cables and a motor doing the lifting.

6. A kettle is a teapot dragon clearing its throat, huffing louder and louder until it screams steam.
   The dragon fits because the whistle grows as the water gets hotter and the steam has to escape somewhere, but it leaves out that dragons aren't real and the whistle is a little valve, not a throat.

7. A fridge light is a tiny sun that only wakes up when you open the door, then pretends to sleep again the moment you close it.
   The sun fits because it switches the dark box bright in an instant, but it leaves out that the bulb is always wired and ready — the door switch just hides it, and it makes no warmth at all.

## Pick

Candidate 1. The sunbed is the most surprising of the seven: a child can
picture bread lying down for a tan, and the pop-up is a punchline built
into the picture. It is one analogy only, no list, no second metaphor.
The second line names the mapping precisely (steady heat + a timer) and
the two real limits (bread has no choice; the glow is wires, not sun),
without praising the analogy. It also stays true: nothing in the picture
gives a 10-year-old the wrong idea about how a toaster works.

Candidate 2 was the runner-up — the condensation correction in its second
line is the most honest of the set — but a sponge is a familiar rain
image, so its first line surprises less than the sunbed.
